A credit-building pipeline that runs through a grocery benefit

For a bank, helping people enroll in SNAP is community benefit. For a mortgage lender it is something more specific: a household sitting at a 634 average credit score is sitting below the line that decides whether they qualify at all. The research above says the binding constraint for many of them is paperwork — and paperwork is the part we fix.

We run factual, geo-targeted outreach to income-eligible households across [[scope]], walk them through an eligibility check, and help them complete and submit the application. The household applies; the state agency decides. We take no position on anything else in their financial life.

What you receive

  • A quarterly report written for your examination file: delivery reconciled to platform invoices, eligibility checks and applications as recorded system events, outcomes from a consented follow-up panel with opt-in and response rates stated, and a page mapping the quarter onto the criteria your examiner applies.
  • A signed one-page qualification memorandum stating the activity, its primary purpose, the statutory basis in each covered state, and the dollars — the record assembled by the grantee rather than reconstructed by your compliance staff at exam time.
  • The shortfalls too. When a funnel step misses its projection, the report says so and says what changes next quarter.

Two things we will not do, stated before you ask

No lead generation. We will not route participants to you, share their information with you, or mention any lender in consumer materials. This is not modesty — an activity that generates the funder’s customers is not an activity whose primary purpose is community development, and the qualification argument would collapse. It follows that this comes from a compliance or community budget rather than a marketing budget, because we cannot deliver what marketing money buys.

No compensation tied to enrollments. Nobody on this program is paid per application or per approval, and the grant is not priced per enrollment.

Why this qualifies

Community services targeted to low- and moderate-income individuals are community development activity under every state community investment regulation that applies to you, and helping an income-qualified household obtain a benefit it is entitled to is such a service. Two things make the argument unusually short here.

Eligibility for this program is itself an income test

Every household served is income-qualified by construction, not inferred from the census tract they live in. That is a stronger evidentiary position than most community development activity can offer, and it means no household-level income documentation is required of you.

A Massachusetts examiner has already credited this activity

In a published Division of Banks performance evaluation, the qualified investments of another mortgage lender were credited by reference to an organization whose programs the examiner described as including “assistance in applying for SNAP (food stamp) benefits.” The activity is already in the Commonwealth’s examination record as creditable community development. We are not asking you to accept a novel reading.

Federally, the Interagency Questions and Answers name the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance program among the government assistance programs whose income qualifications are equivalent to or stricter than the CRA definitions of low- and moderate-income — the agencies’ own list, used by their examiners. State regulations adopt the same community development category. Full citations available on request.

What we do not claim

  • No agency pre-certifies a third party’s activity. The determination is yours, documented by you, reviewed at examination. Our job is to make that documentation easy and well-evidenced. Anyone promising you an outcome is selling something.
  • Nothing here is a representation about how any examiner will weigh this activity, or about your rating.
  • Where a state applies an optional investment test, we say so rather than implying a requirement.

Methodology and known limits

  • Need estimates use the 130% gross-income screen, which ignores asset tests, and ACS respondents under-report benefit receipt. Both push the estimate upward. Read as an upper-range estimate of unmet need.
  • Origination counts are 2024 HMDA (the most recent full filing year); each statute tests the prior calendar year, so your covered status should be confirmed against current data.
  • The credit research is preliminary and under journal review; we cite direction and the authors’ own figures, never our own estimates, and never a claim about an individual applicant.
  • Assumption set with source citations, IRS determination letter, budget, board and officer list, and state charitable-solicitation registration all available on request.
