What SNAP is, and why eligible residents miss out

SNAP (food stamps) is monthly grocery money for low- and moderate-income households. Most who qualify but never enroll are stopped by the process, not the rules—so Demeter, Civica Torrey's free online platform for [[program_ref]], answers their questions in plain language and guides them through the application.

A benefits-access campaign for [[bank_name]]

The campaign. It pairs the reach of digital outreach with the accuracy of a one-on-one assister, and every answer traces to the governing rule. Demeter provides free [[program_ref]] application assistance—what a county-designated assister or food-bank navigator gives—across your whole assessment area, geo-targeted to the highest-need tracts. At near-zero marginal cost, one grant funds it area-wide.

For [[bank_name]]'s community-reinvestment file
Primary purposeThe grant funds only [[program_ref]] application assistance—a community-development service to LMI individuals ([[cra_rule_cite]]). It qualifies on the activity itself; the results below only strengthen the file.
LMI targetingTwo independent tests. Delivery: the share of digital-outreach delivery and eligibility-check sessions in LMI tracts, reported quarterly. Eligibility: the program's gross-income ceiling (200% of the federal poverty level) sits within the LMI band (80% or less of area median income) for households of up to five in all four counties. The federal Interagency Q&A (§ __.12(g)(2)–1; 81 FR 48506, 2016) also treats SNAP recipients as LMI, and applicants become recipients on enrollment.
Geographic nexusDelivered and measured within [[bank_name]]'s delineated assessment area, documented by county. Where banks share geography, credit is allocated by each bank's share of dollars in each county, so nothing is claimed twice.
Investment-test criteriaRelevant to the qualitative factors at [[cra_invest_cite]]. Innovativeness (e)(2): measured, attributable results, not activity counts. Responsiveness (e)(3): addresses the counties' documented access barriers. (e)(4): not routinely provided by private investors.
DocumentationA quarterly report of outreach delivery, eligibility checks, and applications started and submitted (all by county; the sample on page 4 shows the format), plus a one-page qualification memo and an annual exam-file summary. Prepared for your file.
Treatment under the CRA framework remains subject to your institution's compliance team and examiners.

The mid-range uses all-user conversion rates, not engaged-user figures. In Civica Torrey's Baker-grant impact study (N=800), applicants with 5+ queries submitted at ~74% and were approved at ~68%. Set version [[assumptions_version]].

Measurement

  • Attribution: neighborhoods launch in waves, so each quarter compares reached tracts against not-yet-reached ones—an attributable estimate, not just a funnel count. Every "recorded" row above is a system event from the assistant on page 3.
  • Follow-up: the figures above model a full quarter; the delivered report measures approvals only among opt-in follow-up respondents, discloses the response rate, and never extrapolates to non-respondents.
  • Payment: never tied to enrollment counts—you fund the outreach and the auditable record, not outcomes.
