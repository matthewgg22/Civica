How measurement works

This pilot's stated learning goal is measuring the step no published benchmark covers: how many residents who start an eligibility check go on to submit an application. The measurement plan, in the order data flows:
  • Layer 1 — Observed (system of record): ad delivery by geography reconciled monthly against invoices; sessions, eligibility checks completed, likely-eligible rate, clarification questions answered, applications started and submitted with assistance — every number in this layer is a recorded system event, and this layer alone carries the CRA documentation (eligibility screening evidences "eligible to receive," the standard the guidance uses).
  • Layer 2 — Consented follow-up panel: users may opt into a 30/60-day check-in ("Did you apply? Were you approved?"). Reported exactly as measured: opt-in rate, response rate, and outcomes among respondents — never silently extrapolated to the full population. Where a range is projected from panel results, the conservative bound is respondents-only.
  • Layer 3 — Aggregate corroboration: the state's published monthly county caseload figures appear alongside our numbers as directional context (not attribution). At multi-county scale, geographic holdout testing becomes available for causal measurement — the phase-two design.
  • Privacy posture: minimum collection, immediate aggregation, follow-up data retained only for the panel window. We report counts, never people.
  • Known limits, stated up front: ZIP codes straddle county lines; some users click from outside the assessment area; session-to-application rates are the pilot's measurement target, not a promise. The first report quantifies each of these rather than hiding them.
Why we report this way. Payment is never tied to enrollment counts, ads are factual rather than persuasive, and projections are labeled until they are measurements. A report your examiner can trust is worth more to both of us than a bigger number.

  • Known bias, disclosed: the model uses a gross-income proxy for SNAP eligibility, and ACS respondents under-report benefit receipt. Both effects push the estimated eligible-unenrolled population upward. Figures here should be read as an upper-range estimate of unmet need.
