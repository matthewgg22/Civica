# Pennsylvania charitable registration — prep

**Prepared 2026-08-25.** Preparation only; the filing is signed and submitted by an officer.
Unlike the Massachusetts research, this one **was read from the source** — the Bureau's own
BCO-10 instructions (REV-2024) at pa.gov.

## What it gates

**$50,000 across two banks — the second-largest state on the board.**

| Bank | Ask | County |
|---|---|---|
| **Meridian Bank** | $25,000 | Philadelphia (11-county, four-state AA) |
| **Firstrust Savings Bank** | $25,000 | Philadelphia (Bucks, Chester, Delaware, Montgomery, Philadelphia + Camden NJ) |

Both solicit into Philadelphia, the 18th largest SNAP enrollment gap in the country, and
Firstrust is the only bank in the roster with **all three tests** rated Low Satisfactory.

## Pennsylvania is the cheapest of the four

The registration fee is banded by **gross annual contributions**, and at Civica Torrey's
current level it sits at the bottom:

| Gross annual contributions | Fee |
|---|---|
| **$25,000 or less** | **$15** |
| $25,001 – under $100,000 | $100 |
| $100,000 – under $500,000 | $150 |
| over $500,000 | $250 |

**Form BCO-10** serves as both initial registration and annual renewal.

## The no-990 path is explicit, and it is BCO-23

The instructions address our exact situation in as many words:

> *"An organization that is not required to file an IRS 990 return or an organization that
> files a 990N, 990EZ or 990PF must file a Pennsylvania public disclosure form (BCO-23)."*

So the missing Form 990 is not an obstacle in Pennsylvania either — **file BCO-23 in its
place**. That is now three of four states where the absent 990 turns out not to block
registration, against a working assumption earlier in this project that it blocked
everything.

## Financial statements — internally prepared is enough for now

| Gross annual contributions | Statement required |
|---|---|
| $25,000 – under $100,000 | **internally prepared** (or compiled/reviewed/audited) |
| $100,000 – under $250,000 | compiled, reviewed or audited |
| $250,000 – under $750,000 | reviewed or audited |
| $750,000 or more | audited |

**No audit is needed to register.** As in Massachusetts, the obligation arrives with
success rather than before it: crossing $100,000 in contributions triggers a compiled
statement, and $250,000 a reviewed one.

## Register before soliciting — settled from the source

> *"Initial registration is required prior to any compensated person soliciting
> contributions on behalf of the charitable organization."*

Note the scope: **compensated** person. Whether an uncompensated officer's outreach falls
outside that is a question for counsel, not for me — the safe and obvious reading is to
register first, and at $15 there is no reason to test the edge.

## First-time filing checklist

1. Form **BCO-10**
2. Fee — **$15** at current contribution levels, payable to "Commonwealth of Pennsylvania"
3. **BCO-23** in place of the IRS 990
4. Initial registrants only: IRS exemption letter · charter or organizational documents · bylaws
5. Financial statement — internally prepared is acceptable at this level

**Renewal** is due by the **15th day of the eleventh month following fiscal year end**, and
the date is printed on the certificate.

## Two things to confirm

1. **Foreign corporation registration.** Civica Torrey is Massachusetts-incorporated.
   Charitable registration with the Bureau is separate from corporate registration with the
   Department of State, and Illinois required *both*. Whether Pennsylvania also expects a
   foreign-nonprofit filing was not answered in the BCO-10 instructions and should be checked.
2. **Whether an exemption applies.** The Bureau publishes an Exclusions and Exemptions Chart
   at dos.pa.gov/charities. Organizations receiving small amounts may be excluded entirely —
   worth two minutes before paying even $15.
