# State CRA regimes and non-bank channels — verification pass

**Verified:** 2026-08-22, against Massachusetts Division of Banks primary sources.
**Question:** does the CRA channel extend beyond federally-examined banks — to credit unions, to
state regimes, to non-bank lenders, to branchless/ILC charters?
**Answer:** yes, but not where we expected. **Massachusetts credit unions are a dead end.
Massachusetts *mortgage lenders* are the real opening**, and the thesis fits them better than it
fits banks.

## 1. Massachusetts credit unions — VERIFIED NEGATIVE

The regime is real. The Division of Banks examines state-chartered credit unions under
**209 CMR 46.00**, with five ratings (MA inserts **High Satisfactory** between Satisfactory and
Outstanding), a published public evaluation per exam, and the federal test structure — lending,
investment, service, **community development test**, strategic plan. CRA performance is weighed on
applications for branches, mergers, acquisitions, and ATMs.

**It fails on two independent grounds.**

**No institution is under pressure.** Parsed from the DOB's consolidated ratings list
([source](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/cra-ratings-and-public-evaluations-for-banks-and-credit-unions)) —
**131 institutions, 45 of them credit unions, and not one rated below Satisfactory:**

| Rating | All institutions | Credit unions |
|---|---|---|
| Outstanding | 18 | 3 |
| High Satisfactory | 24 | 11 |
| Satisfactory | 89 | 31 |
| **Needs to Improve** | **0** | **0** |
| **Substantial Noncompliance** | **0** | **0** |

The channel runs on distress. Massachusetts has none.

**The assessment area may not be geographic.** Per the DOB: *"Massachusetts-chartered credit unions
whose membership by-law provisions are not based on residence may use their membership as the
assessment area."* Our deliverable assumes a geographic AA — county-grain unmet need, a map,
geo-targeted delivery, county reporting. For a membership-based credit union there is no county to
target. The artifact would need rebuilding, not reconfiguring.

Add small institutions (grant budgets at or below Helm's) and a 45-institution universe examined
roughly every three years, and the expected value is close to zero.

**Verdict: do not build. Revisit only if a specific relationship opens a door — and pitch community
partnership, not compliance.** Exam dates are public, and several credit unions are 4–5 years stale
(Athol 2/2021, Align 7/2021, Brotherhood 9/2021, Jeanne D'Arc 12/2021, Alden 2/2022, Liberty Bay
4/2022, Luso-American 9/2022, First Priority 12/2022). Need fit is genuinely strong in a few
places — **Metro CU (Chelsea), BrightBridge (Lawrence), Holyoke CU, Freedom and Greater Springfield
(Springfield)** sit in the state's highest-poverty cities.

**Not verified:** 209 CMR 46's community development definition, and whether it carries the federal
LMI-proxy language naming SNAP. The regulation PDF is behind a mass.gov download that blocks
scripted retrieval and triggers a save dialog in-browser. MA's rule is modeled on the federal one so
it very likely does — but it is unread, and moot for the go/no-go.

## 2. Massachusetts mortgage lenders — VERIFIED POSITIVE, and the better fit

**M.G.L. c. 255E § 8 · 209 CMR 54.00 · "Mortgage Lender Community Investment."** The DOB examines
every licensed mortgage lender making **50 or more home mortgage loans in Massachusetts** in the
prior calendar year. Same five ratings, same published public evaluations.

**Seven lenders currently carry a Needs to Improve rating** — against zero banks and zero credit
unions. From the mortgage lender ratings list
([source](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/cra-ratings-and-public-evaluations-for-mortgage-lenders)),
99 institutions rated: 85 Satisfactory, 7 High Satisfactory, **7 Needs to Improve**, zero Outstanding.

| Lender | HQ | Exam date | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provident Funding Associates, LP | Santa Rosa CA | 8/31/2023 | NI |
| Toll Brothers Mortgage Company | Fort Washington PA | 12/18/2023 | NI |
| Poli Mortgage Group, Inc. | Norwood **MA** | 2/3/2023 | NI |
| MLD Mortgage, Inc. | Florham Park NJ | 10/31/2022 | NI |
| FBC Mortgage, LLC | Orlando FL | 9/19/2022 | NI |
| Northeast Home Loan, LLC | West Chesterfield NH | 11/15/2021 | NI |
| MiLend, Inc. | Atlanta GA | 5/10/2017 | NI |

**The DOB publishes a forward exam schedule with named institutions and quarters**
([source](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/cra-examination-schedule-for-mortgage-lenders)) — the
same "book a grant into the evaluation period" lever the federal channel uses:

- **Q3 2026 (live now):** Ark-La-Tex Financial · Envoy Mortgage · **Fairway Independent Mortgage** ·
  **Guaranteed Rate** · Plaza Home Mortgage
- **Q2 2026:** American Neighborhood Mortgage · Nations Direct · Radius Financial (Norwell MA) ·
  Total Mortgage Services
- **Q1 2026:** AmWest Funding · First Home Mortgage · Freedom Mortgage · New American Funding
- Recently examined: Rocket Mortgage, PennyMac, NewRez (all Q4 2025)

**Fairway Independent Mortgage is a double signal** — MA exam in Q3 2026 *and* an active
~$9.9M DOJ redlining consent order in Birmingham (see the national target list). It is the only
institution appearing on both our federal and state lists.

### Why this fits better than banks

**The assessment area is the entire Commonwealth.** Per the DOB, *"The Commonwealth of Massachusetts
is the assessment area for mortgage lenders"* (a lender may request a different one). That erases
the county-precision problem outright — no map, no per-county attribution, no overlapping-AA
double-counting, and **no exposure to Meta's special-ad-category radius floor**, since statewide
targeting doesn't need ZIP granularity. We already hold an MA county fact base.

**The credit thesis is first-order here, not analogous.** For a bank, "SNAP access raises credit
scores" is a community-benefit argument. For a **mortgage lender**, it is a pipeline argument: a
household moving 17 points off a 634 average is moving toward the boundary that decides whether they
qualify at all. This is the unbankable-to-qualified-borrower thesis delivered to the institution that
directly captures the upside. NBER WP 34434 was always going to land hardest here.

**These are large, marketing-fluent originators.** Rocket, PennyMac, Freedom, Guaranteed Rate,
Fairway, New American Funding run nine-figure marketing budgets. They understand paid acquisition
natively, which is the language our deliverable already speaks.

### The catches — real, and they cut

- **The investment test is OPTIONAL for mortgage lenders** (tests are lending, service, and
  investment-at-election). A grant is therefore not required. Cuts both ways: it is weaker as an
  obligation, but it is one of the few *discretionary* levers a lender with a weak lending record can
  pull without changing how it lends. That is the pitch to the seven NI lenders.
- **No Outstanding ratings exist in the entire population** — the ceiling appears to be High
  Satisfactory. Never imply a grant produces an Outstanding.
- **Massachusetts is not wired into the generator's state registry**, despite our having MA data.
- **MA charitable-solicitation registration (Form PC + Schedule A-2, ~$150) legally gates
  solicitation in MA** and is unfiled. This channel cannot open before that does.
- The 209 CMR 54 community development definition is unread, same download block as above.

**Verdict: pursue.** Highest-value next step is reading the seven NI public evaluations to find the
documented gap — the same PE-mining discipline that corrected the bank asks.

## 3. Other state CRA regimes — ASSERTED, NOT VERIFIED

⚠️ **Everything in this section is my own prior claim carried forward without primary-source
verification. Treat as a research to-do list, not as findings.** The WP 34434 episode is the standing
reminder: state it as unverified or verify it.

- **Illinois** — the 2021 Illinois CRA reportedly covers state-chartered banks, **credit unions, and
  mortgage licensees**, administered by IDFPR. If accurate this is the broadest state regime in the
  country and the only other one covering credit unions. **Verify first** — implementation timing and
  rulemaking status are unknown to me.
- **New York, Connecticut, Rhode Island, West Virginia** — state CRA regimes of varying scope,
  believed to cover banks rather than credit unions. Unverified.

New York is worth checking despite the CHAR410 registration burden, given the borough need figures
(Kings 205K, Queens 190K, Bronx 110K) and that NY is already wired in the generator.

## 4. Credit unions outside a state CRA — philanthropic, not compliance

Federal CRA applies to FDIC-insured banks and thrifts. Federally chartered credit unions answer to
NCUA, which has no CRA equivalent — no exam, no rating, no assessment area obligation. **There is no
compliance lever.**

They do have a live motivation: the credit union tax exemption faces recurring political attack, and
credit unions actively document community impact to defend it. But that is a soft, slow sell with no
exam date creating urgency. Different pitch, weaker close. Not a priority.

## 5. Digital banks and ILCs — in the list, and underweighted

Already present in the national target list but never ranked: **Sallie Mae · Celtic · LendingClub
(Lehi UT) · Varo (SF, OCC 04) · Ameriprise FSB · Redemption**, plus Customers Bank, MVB,
**Forbright ($5.4B, NTI)**, **GBank ($1.2B Las Vegas, NTI)**, and the Enova→Grasshopper merger.

**The structural argument for them is stronger than the list reflects.** A branchless ILC has no
retail network, so it cannot tell a branch-based lending story. Its assessment area is typically the
single county where it is chartered — Salt Lake County for the Utah ILCs. It has real capital and
real CRA obligations, and almost no natural way to satisfy them locally.

**Grants and qualified investments are its primary lever, in one county, by structural necessity.**
That is exactly what we sell. Every other institution on the target list can choose between lending,
investments, and services. An ILC largely cannot. The national doc noted this in a single line —
*"CRA strategy = grants (no branch lending story); unconventional but money-rich"* — and then left
them unranked. That was a mistake.

**The catch:** Salt Lake County shows only ~13K unserved households on the screening index, so the
need story is weak, and Utah is not wired into the generator. The pitch inverts — lead with fit and
ease of compliance rather than magnitude of need. That is a different document than the one we built.

**Best single target regardless of geography: GBank** (~$1.2B, Las Vegas, current NTI). Fintech-forward,
NTI-rated, single-metro assessment area in Clark County — the ILC structural advantage *plus* a
pressure signal. Nevada is one `build_state.py` run from a fact base.

## What changed as a result of this pass

1. **Massachusetts credit unions: closed.** Negative result recorded so nobody re-runs it.
2. **Massachusetts mortgage lenders: opened**, with seven named NI institutions, a live Q3 2026 exam
   schedule, a statewide assessment area that sidesteps three of our hardest problems, and the
   credit-score thesis landing as a pipeline argument rather than a charitable one.
3. **Digital banks/ILCs promoted** from an appendix line to a ranked band.
4. **Illinois and the other state regimes** moved onto an explicit verify-before-citing list.
