# The other three mortgage-lender jurisdictions — checked

**Checked:** 2026-08-22 against IDFPR, NYDFS and DISB primary sources.
**Context:** [state-cra-and-nonbank-channels-2026-08-22.md](state-cra-and-nonbank-channels-2026-08-22.md) ·
[ma-lender-pe-full-scan-2026-08-22.md](ma-lender-pe-full-scan-2026-08-22.md)

Four jurisdictions reach mortgage lenders: **DC, Illinois, Massachusetts, New York.** Massachusetts
is done — 99 evaluations read, ~40 Service Test failures. This checks the other three.

**Result: Massachusetts is the only one with published evidence to target. The other three are
pre-examination clean slates.**

---

## Illinois — the program is real but has published almost nothing

The Illinois CRA (205 ILCS 735, signed March 2021, rules effective **May 1, 2024**) is confirmed to
cover *"state-chartered banks, credit unions, and non-bank mortgage licensees"* — my earlier claim
holds. Two divisions examine: the **Division of Banking** takes banks and covered mortgage lenders,
the **Division of Financial Institutions** takes credit unions.

**Mortgage lenders — nothing published.** IDFPR's
[Mortgage Lenders Public Evaluations](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/il-cra/mortgage-lenders-public-evaluations.html)
page carries an empty table under the heading **"INFORMATION COMING SOON!"** No Illinois mortgage
lender has a published evaluation. The only mortgage document IDFPR hosts is a blank
*evaluation template*.

**Credit unions — four evaluations, all Satisfactory.** From
[Credit Union Public Evaluations](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/il-cra/cu-public-evaluations.html):

| Credit union | Location | Exam | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| University of Illinois Community CU | Champaign | 12/22/2025 | Satisfactory |
| Healthcare Associates CU | Naperville | 11/24/2025 | Satisfactory |
| Abbott Laboratories Employees CU | Gurnee | 10/23/2025 | Satisfactory |
| Heartland CU | Springfield | 4/15/2025 | Satisfactory |

**No Needs to Improve anywhere.** Same outcome as Massachusetts credit unions, for a different
reason — there the population was clean, here the program has barely started.

Rating scales differ by institution type, worth knowing before quoting one: banks and mortgage
lenders get **Outstanding / Low Satisfactory / Needs to Improve / Substantial Noncompliance** (no
plain "Satisfactory" tier), credit unions get **Outstanding / Satisfactory / NI / SNC**.

## New York — the obligation exists, the evidence does not

NYDFS finalized its implementing regulation 1/7/2026, **effective 7/7/2026** — seven weeks ago. It
covers DFS-licensed non-depository mortgage bankers originating 200+ HMDA-reportable New York loans,
and evaluates HMDA loan distribution plus *"participation in community development-related services."*

**No lender has been examined, so there are no evaluations, no ratings, and no documented gaps.** The
[70-lender target list](../../data-ops/analysis/state-cra-mortgage/ny_nondepository_200plus_2024.csv)
is ranked on origination volume because that is the only signal that exists.

---

## District of Columbia — broadest on paper, thinnest in practice

The CFPB survey records DC as the widest scope of any jurisdiction: banks, credit unions, mortgage
companies **and** non-depositories. But the same survey notes DC *"only applies an investment test to
institutions that hold public deposits"* — which excludes essentially every non-depository mortgage
lender from the test where a grant would count.

DISB licenses non-depository mortgage lenders and brokers, but I found **no published CRA
evaluations or ratings** for them. **Deprioritise.** Revisit only if a DC relationship opens
independently.

---

## What this means

**Massachusetts is the channel.** It is the only jurisdiction with a mature examination record, and
that record is unusually favourable: ~40 lenders with a documented Service Test failure, examiners
repeatedly naming *"financial literacy education initiatives targeted to LMI individuals"* as the
remedy, and a published evaluation already crediting a lender for funding SNAP application
assistance. Nothing in New York, Illinois or DC comes close, because nothing has been published.

**But the clean slates are a different opportunity, not a worse one.** In Massachusetts you sell
against a documented failure. In New York and Illinois you sell against a **brand-new obligation
nobody has a program for** — no incumbent partner to displace, no established vendor, and a first
examination coming that every covered lender knows is coming. That is a real pitch; it simply cannot
be built from evaluation documents, so it needs different collateral.

**Practical sequence:**

1. **Massachusetts now** — evidence in hand, artifact identical across 40 targets, one registration.
2. **New York next** — largest need in our data (505K across three boroughs), obligation seven weeks
   old, but CHAR410 registration is the known-onerous one and gates everything.
3. **Illinois after** — watch the Mortgage Lenders Public Evaluations page. The moment it populates,
   Illinois becomes a second Massachusetts, and Cook County need is substantial.
4. **DC — skip.**

**One monitoring task worth setting:** IDFPR's mortgage evaluations page and NYDFS's first
examination cycle are both leading indicators. Whoever publishes first creates the next documented
gap list, and being early there is worth more than being thorough anywhere else.
