# Does this qualify for CRA credit?

**The answer a CRA officer needs, with the citations and the examiner precedent behind it.**
Prepared 2026-08-26. Every quotation below was read from the primary document, not a summary.

---

## The 30-second answer

Yes, on two of the three tests, and the agencies have already named our program category
by name.

A grant to Civica Torrey Inc is a **qualified investment** under **§__.12(t)**, because its
primary purpose is **community development** under **§__.12(g)(2)** — *community services
targeted to low- or moderate-income individuals*. Bank staff time spent on referral or
outreach alongside it is a **community development service** under **§__.12(i)**.

The Interagency Questions and Answers name Supplemental Nutrition Assistance among the
government programs whose recipients qualify as LMI for this purpose. **This is not an
analogy we are drawing; it is the agencies' own example.**

It moves the **Investment Test** and the **Service Test**. It does not move the Lending
Test, and we never claim it does.

---

## The controlling citation, verbatim

**Interagency Questions and Answers Regarding Community Reinvestment**, 81 Fed. Reg. 48506
(July 25, 2016), § __.12(g)(2)—1, answering how an institution may determine that a
community service is targeted to LMI individuals. Among the listed examples:

> "The community service is provided to recipients of government assistance programs that
> have income qualifications equivalent to, or stricter than, the definitions of low- and
> moderate-income as defined by the CRA Regulations. Examples include U.S. Department of
> Housing and Urban Development's section 8, 202, 515, and 811 programs or U.S. Department
> of Agriculture's section 514, 516, and **Supplemental Nutrition Assistance programs**."

The agencies made the income-threshold determination themselves, at the federal program
level. A bank does not have to re-derive whether SNAP's limits sit at or below LMI — the
Q&A already treats the answer as settled.

**Which framework applies.** The 2016 Q&As interpret the long-standing CRA regulations
(12 CFR Parts 25, 228, 345). We confirmed empirically rather than assuming: of the seven
performance evaluations in our corpus **dated 2026**, every one applies the *Interagency
Large* or *Intermediate Small Institution Examination Procedures* and recites the classic
four-prong community development definition. None uses 2023-rule vocabulary. The framework
cited here is the one examiners are actually applying right now.

## The honest wrinkle, and why it does not bite

A careful reader will notice that the SNAP bullet says **"recipients of"** government
assistance programs — while the Medicaid bullet just above it says "receive **or are
eligible to receive**." Much of our work reaches households who are *eligible but not yet
enrolled*. On a strict reading, that bullet alone would not cover them.

**We do not need that bullet to carry the argument.** The same Q&A offers other independent
routes, and two of them fit our population exactly:

> "The community service is targeted to the clients of a nonprofit organization that has a
> defined mission of serving low- and moderate-income persons…"

> "The community service is a clearly defined program that benefits primarily low- or
> moderate-income persons, even if it is provided by an entity that offers other programs
> that serve individuals of all income levels."

Our mission is defined that way and our program is defined that way. The SNAP bullet is
then corroboration on the income threshold rather than the load-bearing element.

**Raise this yourself before they do.** A CRA officer who spots the distinction after you
have leaned on the SNAP bullet will discount everything else you said. One who watches you
name it and route around it will trust the rest of the memo.

## Examiner precedent

We built a corpus of **120 CRA performance evaluations** and searched it. **36 of them —
30% — contain food-security activity sitting in a CRA-credit context.** This is ordinary,
routinely-credited activity, not a novel theory.

### A grant to a food-security nonprofit, credited as a community service to LMI

**The Village Bank** (Newton, MA · cert 26336 · PE May 20, 2024):

> "Newton Food Pantry – VB granted a total of $71,000 over the evaluation period to the
> Newton Food Pantry. The bank's contributions support a community development service to
> low- and moderate-income individuals and help to alleviate food insecurity in Newton."

That is the whole argument in one examiner sentence: **money to a food-security
organisation → community development service to LMI individuals → credited.**

### An entire assessment area's Investment Test carried by food-pantry grants

**First Citizens Bank** (Mason City, IA · RSSD 186744 · PE January 26, 2026 — the most
recent exam in our corpus):

> "The bank distributed a total of $70,500 in qualified grants and donations in the current
> evaluation period… **All the grants were distributed to organizations providing community
> services, such as food pantries.**"

### More of the same, across regulators and regions

| Institution | PE date | What the examiner credited |
|---|---|---|
| **Bank of Guam** (cert 20884) | Mar 17, 2025 | "4 donations totaling $55,000 to an organization that supported a food bank and other community services for LMI individuals" |
| **First Western Bank & Trust** (cert 19123) | Sep 23, 2024 | "donations totaling $8,460 to an organization focused on fighting poverty, homelessness, and food insecurity in the AA" |
| **Bank of Tennessee** (cert 21573) | May 13, 2024 | "two donations totaling $3,000 to a local non-profit… food pantry providing free food to LMI families, **providing a community service to LMI individuals**" |
| **The Fidelity Bank** (cert 11507) | — | "$500 in donations to a local food pantry providing food to **meet basic nutritional needs** for individuals and families in need" |
| **Shinhan Bank America** (cert 33188) | Jul 29, 2024 | credited a site containing a WIC office providing "special supplemental nutrition programs for low-income individuals" |

Note the range: **$500 to $71,000**, at banks from $2.0B to $4.7B in assets. Our ask sits
inside this band at every bank on the roster. Nobody is being asked for an unusual number.

### Staff time, credited under the Service Test

**F.N.B. Corporation** (OCC · PE August 26, 2024):

> "A bank employee provided 30 hours serving as co-chair of the board for a local community
> service organization. The organization assists in the issue of food insecurity for LMI
> families in the surrounding area by providing a food pantry and assistance with utilities
> and medicine."

**Texas Exchange Bank** (PE June 5, 2023) — the strongest precedent for a *service*
partnership rather than a cheque:

> "The bank successfully received 25 grants totaling $254 thousand for five of the CD
> organizations with whom it collaborates. Since the previous CRA evaluation, the bank
> applied for 140 grants on behalf of organizations that provide community services like
> childcare, **food pantries**, and shelters."

The examiner treated that as **innovative or complex CD services**. A bank whose gap is on
the Service Test, not the Investment Test, should read that paragraph closely.

### On the LMI status of benefit recipients

**Northfield Bank** (PE December 4, 2006 — old, and offered only for the proposition):

> "…food stamps and cash benefits are delivered electronically to recipients via a New York
> State issued debit card… **Recipients of this program are exclusively low- and
> moderate-income.**"

Cite this sparingly and always with its date. It is a clean statement of the principle, but
a twenty-year-old evaluation is corroboration, not authority. The 2016 Q&A is the authority.

## ⚠️ The trap that will destroy your credibility

**In bank CRA evaluations, "SNAP" usually does not mean food assistance.** It is the
Federal Home Loan Bank's **Special Needs Assistance Program**, a home-repair subsidy. In
our corpus, **four of the seven literal "SNAP" hits were the FHLB program**, appearing in
Woodforest, Texas Capital and First Bank evaluations.

Never cite a bank's own PE back to it as "you already fund SNAP" without opening the
document and reading which SNAP it means. A CRA officer will know instantly, and everything
else in the packet becomes suspect. We nearly made this error while building this pack.

## Objections, and how to answer them

**"Isn't this just charity? Where is the community development purpose?"**
The primary-purpose test is met on the face of the program: the service is a defined program
benefiting primarily LMI persons, delivered by a nonprofit whose defined mission is serving
them. That satisfies §__.12(g)(2) by two independent routes in the Q&A.

**"Our CRA dollars go to affordable housing."**
Housing is one of four prongs, not the definition. In the corpus, community services to LMI
individuals frequently *dominate* a bank's CD service mix — 83.5% at City Bank, 59.7% at
another. Examiners repeatedly describe community services as "an identified need."

**"Show me a bank that got credit for this."**
The Village Bank paragraph, above. Then First Citizens, January 2026.

**"Does it count if we do not lend against it?"**
Yes. A grant is a qualified investment under §__.12(t) regardless of any credit
relationship, and staff hours are a CD service under §__.12(i).

**"Will it count in our assessment area?"**
Only if the beneficiaries are in it. Every artifact we send is built against **the bank's
own assessment area as stated in its own public evaluation** — never a branch-inferred or
marketing-derived footprint. If the geography does not work, we say so and do not send.

**"We are examined under Small Bank procedures."**
Then it is genuinely weaker for you — Small Bank is a Lending Test only, with no Investment
or Service component to move. We track exam method per institution for exactly this reason
and do not pitch the CRA angle where it cannot land.

## What we do not claim

- **It does not move the Lending Test**, which carries the most weight in the overall rating.
- **It does not guarantee a rating change.** It is one item in a mix, and examiners weigh
  responsiveness, innovation, complexity and dollar volume.
- **It does not qualify automatically outside the assessment area** (broader statewide or
  regional treatment is a separate analysis, and we do not assert it).
- **We are not your counsel.** Every citation here is checkable, and your CRA officer should
  check them. That is why the sources are named and dated rather than paraphrased.
