## Why this email exists

The full working paper is **not gated after all** — Katherine Meckel hosts an open PDF on her UCSD
faculty site ([sites.google.com/view/katherinemeckel](https://sites.google.com/view/katherinemeckel/home),
linked as `SNAP_CCP-19.pdf`, dated November 3 2025, ~1.1 MB). NBER's own copy is paywalled; the
author's copy is not. That is normal and it is how the authors intend the work to circulate.

**But the draft's title page carries a standing instruction: _"PRELIMINARY—PLEASE DO NOT CITE OR
DISTRIBUTE."_** The paper is under Revise & Resubmit at *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy*,
so the estimates can still move between now and publication.

That instruction governs. It means:

- ✅ **Still fine to cite:** the paper's existence, authorship, design, and the *qualitative* finding,
  because NBER publishes the abstract openly and NYU issued a press release about it. Public
  statements by the authors and their institution are public.
- ❌ **Not fine to publish:** any effect magnitude read out of the draft — credit-score points,
  dollar figures, percentage-point changes, or the paper's own translation of score gains into
  borrower savings. Those live only in the preliminary draft.

This matters more here than in ordinary writing. Our artifacts go into a **bank's CRA examination
file**. A number that later moves in the published version, sitting in an exam file under our name,
is a credibility problem for the bank and for us. The conservative read is the correct one.

**So there are exactly two ways to get magnitudes into the pitch:** the authors say yes, or the
AEJ:Policy version publishes. This email pursues the first and costs nothing.

## What to ask for

1. Permission to cite specific estimates from the current draft in materials shown to bank CRA
   officers — and if yes, **which** figures they consider stable enough to quote.
2. Whether they would rather we wait for the published version, and roughly when that is expected.
3. Whether they want to see how their work is being characterized before it goes out. Offering this
   is both courteous and self-serving: a researcher's correction is cheaper than a bank's.

Do **not** ask for the PDF. We already have it, and asking for something already public reads as
either careless or as a pretext.

## What unlocks if they say yes

The draft contains precisely the two things the pitch is currently missing: a credit-score effect
expressed in points, and the authors' own translation of score movement into annual dollar savings
for borrowers. The second is the sentence a bank CRA officer would actually repeat to a credit
committee. Neither is usable today.

Nothing in the pipeline is blocked on this. The channel already works on the qualitative finding —
this would sharpen the argument, not enable it. Send the email, keep shipping, and treat any grant
as an upgrade rather than a dependency.
