# PE Giving Intelligence — what the eight target banks actually give

All seven published Performance Evaluations for the loaded target banks were read in full (Bank Irvine's newest exam is not yet published; its size class is documented below). Every figure is verbatim from the PE. This replaces the asset-size ask heuristics in `tools/cra-artifact/inputs/assessment_areas.json` with each bank's own disclosed giving history.

**The single biggest correction: Helm.** Its entire grant program across 3.2 years was **$25,161**. My $30K ask would have exceeded everything the bank has ever granted — the fastest way to be dismissed as someone who didn't read the file. Sponsor tier only.

**Hanmi has a hard ceiling.** The largest single grant disclosed anywhere in its PE is $15,000, and LA giving *fell* from $519,840 (52 grants) to $281,080 (14 grants). Ask at the ceiling, not above it.

**Two banks have demonstrated six-figure single-grantee capacity** — Five Star ($900K to one school) and ABB ($458K to one CDC). They are the only credible large-ask targets in the set, and ABB's grants grew 185.7% while "doubling the grants at one comparable bank and tripling the grants at the other."

## Three findings that change the pitch

### 1. The NTI banks did not fail on giving — they failed on lending

Helm's Community Development Test was **Satisfactory**; Banco do Brasil's Investment Test was **High Satisfactory**. Both still landed at Needs to Improve because the Lending Test, weighted heaviest, found poor geographic and borrower distribution driven by the same business model: mortgage lending to affluent non-resident aliens buying second homes. Helm's PE says it outright — "The poor performance is partly influenced by the bank's business strategy of lending to affluent non-resident aliens."

**Honest consequence: a grant does not fix their rating, and we must never imply it does.** What is true: they cannot easily change their lending model, so community development is the lever they *can* pull — and for Helm (an intermediate small bank) the CD Test is half the rating, with CD services already flagged "below the performance of similarly situated institutions."

### 2. No examiner and no community contact in any PE named food security, SNAP, or benefits access as a need

Across seven evaluations and roughly a dozen community contacts, the identified needs are consistently: **affordable housing, an LMI-tailored mortgage product, financial literacy education, and small-business technical assistance.**

**So the pitch is framed as community services to LMI individuals plus financial capability/navigation — not food security.** That is simultaneously the CD category the grant dollars actually flow to (Community Services took 18/19 of Helm's grants, 48/60 of BBA's, 84% of Ocean's grant dollars, $1.3M of Bank of Marin's) and the vocabulary the banks' own examiners used. Each bank entry now carries a `pe_need_quote` — its own examination's language — and the qualification memo quotes it back in the responsiveness section.

### 3. Paid service delivery earns favorable treatment — cite the precedent

Five Star's examiners praised a **contracted professional** providing free counseling: *"This activity was especially innovative."* That is direct precedent that a bank-funded professional service counts favorably as a CD service, not just employee volunteer hours. It supports pitching "fund navigator capacity" over "send your staff to volunteer."

## Per-bank openings the PEs handed us

- **Ocean Bank:** the Fort Lauderdale/Broward assessment area received **zero grants and zero CD services** benefiting only that AA last cycle, while examiners wrote that Broward's 39.6% LMI family share and poverty rate "indicate needs for financial literacy." Broward holds ~169K eligible-unenrolled in our data. That is the most specific single opening in the entire target set.
- **Banco do Brasil:** already funds the closest peer programs to ours — six donations totaling **$20,500** to a nonprofit providing "crisis care, food and household subsidy, and employment training," and five totaling **$10,000** to one providing "emergency food and shelter, rent and utility payment assistance, and financial literacy education." Also: its Orlando AA recorded **one** CD service all period.
- **Bank of Marin:** its entire $46.1M investment book is affordable-housing MBS with **zero community-services investments** — every non-housing dollar it deployed came through the grant line. CD service hours fell 17% and sit "below the ratio range of similarly situated institutions." Low Satisfactory on all three tests.
- **Hanmi:** Investment Test "rarely in a leadership position," giving declining, and $3,116 of its 3,496 CD service hours are already in Community Services — the category we occupy.
- **Bank Irvine (honest downgrade):** it is examined under **Small Bank procedures** — a streamlined lending test where CD activity is optional and counts only toward an *Outstanding*. Its NTI is a lending-distribution failure a grant cannot repair. Strongest pressure signal, weakest structural fit; treat as a long-shot sponsorship, not a lead target.

## Method note

FDIC PEs **anonymize every grantee** — across seven evaluations not one recipient organization is named (only "a CDC," "a Title I school," "a non-profit organization that provides…"). PEs therefore supply grant *sizes*, *categories*, and *examiner-identified needs*, but they cannot produce a peer-grantee target list. The only named organizations across all seven are intermediaries: **Neighborhood Lending Partners of Florida**, funded by all three Miami banks, plus Center for Financial Training International and Miami-Dade County Public Schools.
