## Stock Yards — the cleanest read so far

Louisville MSA AA: *"all portions of Bullitt, Jefferson, Oldham, and Shelby Counties in
Kentucky, and Clark and Floyd Counties in Indiana."* Its own AA table gives **Qualified
Grants & Donations of 110 items totaling $805,000** against $121.2M of investments.

Worth noting the AA **outperforms the institution**: the PE says *"The Service Test is rated
Outstanding for this AA"* while the bank overall is High Satisfactory. Reading the
institution grid would have understated it.

## Bank OZK — a service partnership with a real per-AA figure

Texas: **Investment Outstanding / Service LOW SATISFACTORY.** Houston MSA AA is **Harris and
Galveston only** — a third distinct Houston delineation, after Truist's nine counties and
Frost's five.

Its Houston activity table gives **Donations 20 / $95,000** against $15,367,000 of
investments — a **162×** ratio. Instrument-heavy with a service finding: archetype 4, priced
on the floor.

**A trap caught by one line.** The Austin MSA AA table sits immediately above the Houston
heading and ends with *"Donations 14 / $128"*. Read straight through, that figure looks like
Houston's. It is Austin's. The rendered artifact was checked: **zero occurrences of 128.**

## German American — branch data is current, evaluations are historical

The ranking put it in **Franklin County, Ohio** with 15 branches. It has 20 Ohio branches,
confirmed against FDIC locations — Columbus, Dublin, Grove City, Reynoldsburg.

**But its April 2024 evaluation has no Ohio assessment area at all.** Its rated areas are
**Indiana and Kentucky**, and the 25 "Columbus" hits in the document are Columbus, *Indiana*.
"Franklin" appears zero times.

The Ohio franchise post-dates the exam. **This is a new failure mode: branch data is current
while PEs are historical, so an acquisition between the two manufactures a phantom target.**
German American is not approachable in Ohio until its next evaluation delineates an Ohio
assessment area.

## WaFd and Pinnacle — recorded as blocked so nobody re-reads them

**WaFd:** the Arizona table's Phoenix row ($13,141K) sits **above** the Qualified Grants &
Donations line and is investments only; grants are reported once for the whole rated area
(100 items / $449K). Identical structure to Glacier.

**Pinnacle:** the Tennessee table gives Nashville MSA as 615 items / $216,580K, **combining
investments and donations with no split**. Donations are broken out only at institution level.

Both are **peers**, and a peer pitch leads with the bank's own assessment-area figure, so
neither can be sent. Both records carry the finding so the read is not repeated.

**A near-miss on Pinnacle:** a "poor level of qualified investments and grants" passage in its
PE belongs to the **Charleston MSA**, not Nashville. Matching the first occurrence would have
attributed a different market's finding to this one.

## The emerging pattern

Across nine banks now read, an assessment-area **donations-only** figure exists in roughly
half. It reliably appears where the AA is **full-scope**, and reliably does not where the AA
is **limited-scope**. Capacity screening therefore works cleanly only on full-scope areas —
which is a structural property of how these documents are written, not a gap to be closed by
looking harder.
