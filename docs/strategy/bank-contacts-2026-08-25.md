## Why the hit rate is low, and what that means

CRA regulations require a bank to publish a *notice* telling the public where to send
comments — they do not require it to name a person. Most banks satisfy the rule with a
role and an address. So a named-officer roster is not something that can be compiled from
the regulatory record; it is per-bank web research with a genuine miss rate.

**The durable channel is the role, not the name.** `CRA@busey.com` will still work when
Matthew Sabatino moves on. Where both exist, use both.

**Three of these banks appear to have open CRA Officer positions** (Ocean, Hanmi, and one
surfaced next to Busey). A vacant CRA seat means a slow response and, sometimes, an
institution that knows it has a problem. Worth knowing before you read silence as a no.

## Two traps worth stating

**Name collisions nearly caught me twice.** A search for Texas First Bank returned a CRA
officer at *First National Bank Texas* — a different institution. First American Bank has
several unrelated namesakes; I confirmed the Elk Grove Village HQ against cert 3657 before
recording David Leeney. Always match HQ city to the cert before trusting a contact.

**Do not send anything yet.** Contacts are the last mile, not the gate. Charitable-
solicitation registration is unfiled in every state we would pitch, and there is no 990 or
audited financial statement — a common hard gate at bank grant intake, before anyone reads
the pitch.

## How to finish this

For each remaining bank: search `"<bank name>" CRA public file`, open the bank's own CRA
page (not a directory or aggregator), and take the comment contact from the CRA notice
itself. Roughly ten minutes each. The bank's own site is the only source worth recording —
everything else goes stale or belongs to a namesake.
