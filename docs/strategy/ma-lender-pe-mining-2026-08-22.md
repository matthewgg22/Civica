# MA mortgage lender PE mining — six target evaluations read

**Read:** 2026-08-22, from the Division of Banks' published performance evaluations.
**Register:** [`data-ops/analysis/state-cra-mortgage/ma_lender_register_2026.csv`](../../data-ops/analysis/state-cra-mortgage/ma_lender_register_2026.csv)
**Context:** [state-cra-mortgage-lender-targets-2026-08-22.md](state-cra-mortgage-lender-targets-2026-08-22.md)

Six evaluations read end to end. **Three findings change the channel, and the ask sizes came down by
roughly half — the same correction PE mining produced for the banks.**

---

## 1. 🏆 A Massachusetts examiner has already credited a lender for funding SNAP enrollment help

From **Fairway Independent Mortgage's** evaluation, in the Qualified Investments section, describing
an organization Fairway funds:

> "**The Open Door Food Pantry** – a non-profit food pantry whose mission is to alleviate the impact
> of hunger in its community service area of Gloucester, Rockport, Manchester-by-the-Sea, Essex, and
> surrounding towns. This organization consists of a food pantry, community meals, a thrift store,
> and a mobile market. The programs include free summer meals for children, **assistance in applying
> for SNAP (food stamp) benefits**, job-training, and a collaborative meals program that helps other
> organizations feed their clients. This organization primarily services LMI individuals."

**We no longer have to argue that SNAP enrollment assistance is qualifying community development
under the Massachusetts MLCI regulation. A Division of Banks examiner wrote it into a published
evaluation, crediting a lender on our target list for exactly this activity.**

This is stronger than the federal position. Federally, the Interagency Q&A names SNAP as an *LMI
proxy* and we argue the activity from there. Here, the activity itself — application assistance — is
already in the record as creditable, about a specific lender, by the regulator who will examine them.

Quote it on every Massachusetts call. It is the single most useful sentence found in this project.

## 2. Guaranteed Rate's foundation is a food-security funder

From **Guaranteed Rate's** evaluation:

> "The Guarantee Rate Foundation (The Foundation) is involved in nationwide initiatives that focus on
> **investments in housing stability and food security.**"

> "During the review period, The Foundation made approximately **$74,824 in donations** to
> organizations within the Commonwealth that meet the definition of community development. The
> Foundation also provided another **$6.7 million to the nationwide organizations Feeding America and
> Baby2Baby.**"

Their stated mission is our two categories, word for word. They already write eight-figure-adjacent
checks into food security nationally, and $74,824 into Massachusetts.

**This reframes the Guaranteed Rate pitch entirely.** It is not primarily a Massachusetts CRA
conversation — it is: *your foundation gives $6.7M a year to food security; SNAP enrollment
assistance is the same mission with a measurable household outcome, and in Massachusetts it also
earns you CRA credit.* That is a national-relationship pitch with a state hook, not a $25K grant ask.

## 3. Two hidden Service Test failures the ratings table does not show

The DOB's public ratings list shows only the overall rating. Component ratings are inside the PEs,
and two of our six carry a **Service Test rated "Needs to Improve"** behind an overall Satisfactory:

| Lender | Overall | Lending Test | **Service Test** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **loanDepot.com** | Satisfactory | Satisfactory | **Needs to Improve** |
| **NewRez** | Satisfactory | Satisfactory | **Needs to Improve** |
| Guaranteed Rate | Satisfactory | Satisfactory | Satisfactory |
| Guaranteed Rate Affinity | Satisfactory | Satisfactory | Satisfactory |
| Fairway Independent | Satisfactory | Satisfactory | Satisfactory |
| Total Mortgage Services | Satisfactory | *(not broken out)* | *(not broken out)* |

The Service Test is where community development **services** are evaluated — precisely what we
supply. **Screening on overall ratings alone would have missed both.** Any future pass on the
remaining 93 lenders must read component ratings, not the table.

## 4. Verbatim examiner criticism — their documented gap, our answer

Same structure that made the bank call guide work: lead with their evaluation's own words.

**Total Mortgage Services** — the strongest gap on the list:

> "Total Mortgage **does not currently engage in any qualified community development investment.**"

> "Management is encouraged to employ a strong focus and a pro-active commitment in community
> development activities or investments... Other activities may include, but not necessarily limited
> to: **financial literacy education initiatives targeted to low and moderate-income individuals**;
> and foreclosure prevention counseling..."

Zero community development investment, an evaluation dated **1/13/2014**, and a **Q2 2026 exam
scheduled**. Twelve years on, with a published finding of nothing and a named remedy.

**loanDepot.com** — Service Test "Needs to Improve":

> "loanDepot provided a **limited level of community development activities** within Massachusetts."

> "Management is **strongly encouraged** to expand its commitment in community development activities
> and investments... Other activities may include... **financial literacy education initiatives
> targeted to LMI individuals**..."

**NewRez** — Service Test "Needs to Improve":

> "NewRez participated in **limited, qualified community development activities** in Massachusetts."

> "Management is encouraged to develop a pro-active commitment in community development..."

**Note what the examiners keep naming as the remedy: "financial literacy education initiatives
targeted to LMI individuals."** That is the category the CRA channel already frames into — community
services plus financial capability — and it is what these three lenders have been told, in writing,
to go do.

## 5. Revised asks — evidence replacing inference

Prior figures were reasoned from volume alone. These are grounded in disclosed giving and documented
gap, and the total fell from **$175,000 to $95,000**.

| Lender | MA orig. | Prior ask | **Revised** | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Guaranteed Rate** | 4,444 | $50,000 | **$25,000** | $74,824 total MA giving in the review period — a $50K single grant would be two-thirds of it. Pitch the national food-security relationship separately and larger. |
| **loanDepot** | 1,206 | — | **$20,000** | Service Test NI + "strongly encouraged" |
| **Fairway** | 2,100 | $35,000 | **$15,000** | No dollar total disclosed; giving pattern is community-scale (food pantry, a basketball court) |
| **NewRez** | 951 | — | **$15,000** | Service Test NI |
| **Total Mortgage Services** | 1,327 | $20,000 | **$10,000** | Zero CD investment history — no giving muscle at all, so start small. Highest urgency, lowest capacity. |
| **Guaranteed Rate Affinity** | 664 | — | **$10,000** | Has CD activity, clean across all tests |
| | | **$175,000** | **$95,000** | |

**Approach order** — by documented gap rather than by size: **Total Mortgage** (zero investment, exam
imminent) → **loanDepot** and **NewRez** (Service Test NI) → **Fairway** (the SNAP precedent is in
their own PE) → **Guaranteed Rate** (different, bigger conversation) → **GR Affinity**.

## Method note

mass.gov's WAF blocks scripted retrieval, and in-browser PDF extraction failed against the page CSP.
These six were downloaded by hand and parsed locally. **The remaining 93 evaluations are unread** —
every PE URL is in the register, and the same manual route works. Given that this pass surfaced two
hidden Service Test failures and one decisive regulatory precedent from six documents, the rest is
likely worth reading.
